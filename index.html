For new Project Managers

Departments and Who to Contact

Starting a new role means new faces, new tools, and plenty of "who do I ask about this?" moments. This guide shows how The Banished is organized, which team owns what, and how fast each lane usually responds.

Not sure who to contact?

Email hr@the-banished.com and HR will point you in the right direction.

01

How We Are Set Up

All departments report to the CEO. As a PM, your daily rhythm will mostly touch Production and Organizational, with Legal, Financial, IT, R&D, and Creative brought in when the work requires them.

      How we recognize

      Going the Extra Mile

      Discretionary bonuses are extra payments on top of regular salary or fee. They are not automatic or guaranteed. The Company may choose to award one when someone has done something genuinely worth recognizing.

      Earn monthly, get paid annually

      Bonus credit can be earned any month of the year. Everything is collected and paid once per year, in October or December, as a year-end thank-you for contributions made along the way.

      01

      The big picture

      Bonuses are open to everyone at The Banished: full-time employees, part-time employees, remote contractors, and freelancers. If you contribute, you are eligible.

      02

      What can earn a bonus

      • Going beyond scope: work clearly outside the original agreement or role, delivered well.
      • Driving a company win: actions, decisions, or creative output that led to measurable revenue, growth, or savings.
      • Doing something remarkable: work that stood out, even if it does not fit a neat category.
      03

      The approval process

      1. Your direct manager spots the contribution and prepares a recommendation.
      2. The recommendation is reviewed through the appropriate leadership chain.
      3. Finance confirms approval and schedules payment for the next available window.

      You do not need to nominate yourself. If something is worth recognizing, your manager will know.

      04

      Payment windows

      October

      If everything is approved and ready, the bonus arrives before the end of October.

      December

      If October is not feasible, Finance will confirm and payment moves to December.

      You will hear from us at least 10 business days before the payment date.

      05

      Taxes and practical notes

      • W-2 employee bonuses go through payroll and taxes are handled automatically.
      • Contractors and freelancers receive the full amount and handle their own taxes.
      • A bonus one year does not guarantee one the next. Each review is independent.
      • The amount is always at the Company's discretion, with no set minimum or maximum.
      Questions? Contact HR at hr@the-banished.com.

      Monthly challenges

      Challenge Bonuses

      Challenge Bonuses are structured monthly opportunities inside the Internal Portal. Each challenge has a published credit value, clear completion expectations, and a simple manager review path.

      Minimum value

      Every published challenge is worth at least 10 bonus credits. One bonus credit equals one US dollar.

      01

      How employees participate

      1. Open Challenge of the Month and choose an active challenge.
      2. Complete the work during the calendar month listed on the card.
      3. Send the completed artifact, link, screenshot, or short summary to your manager.
      4. Your manager reviews usefulness, completeness, and whether the work matches the challenge brief.
      02

      What counts as complete

      A complete challenge should be specific, usable, and easy for another person to understand. Strong submissions include context, the work itself, the business or team impact, and any suggested next step.

      03

      Approval and credits

      Approved work is recorded as bonus credits in the employee profile. Admins can update the credit balance after manager confirmation, correction, or payroll reconciliation.

      Portal credits

      Credits That Convert to Money

      Internal Portal credits are the running record of approved challenge bonuses and portal-based reward opportunities. They make small contributions visible before they are converted into cash.

      Conversion rate

      One bonus credit equals one US dollar. For example, 45 approved credits equals 45 dollars.

      01

      Where credits appear

      Employees can see their current approved credit balance in Profile. Admins can edit credits in the Users and permissions table when a manager approves new credits or a correction is needed.

      02

      How credits become money

      1. Credits are earned through approved portal challenges or other approved portal reward programs.
      2. Admin records the credits in the employee profile.
      3. Finance reviews the credit balance before the next approved payout window.
      4. Approved credits are paid through the appropriate payroll, contractor, or vendor payment process.
      03

      Important notes

      • Credits are not cash until approved and processed by Finance.
      • Credits may be adjusted if a duplicate, error, or ineligible submission is found.
      • Taxes, withholding, and payment timing depend on worker type, location, and applicable law.

      Culture

      The Banished Culture

      We want the right people

      We empower human stories through technology and film. The culture is global by nature, human by heart, creatively free, technically responsible, and always in progress.

      IIntegrityDo what is right, even when it is hard.
      MMindsetGrowth is our engine.
      PPeopleGreat people make great companies.
      AAdaptabilityStay sharp by staying flexible.

      01

      What guides our work

      Storytelling that matters

      Every product, decision, or scene should leave something or someone better than before.

      Technology that liberates

      Technology should create room for better human work, not erase emotion, vision, or soul.

      A culture that listens

      We ask, clarify, improve, and make space for different ways of processing and contributing.

      02

      How we operate

      • English is the main language, but inclusion matters more than uniformity.
      • We assume good intent, clarify before escalating, and ask before judging.
      • Async-first, sync when needed. Clear is kind. Feedback is not an attack.
      • No meetings for the sake of meetings. Call one when it moves things forward.
      03

      Mental health and emotional safety

      Balance matters

      We do not glorify burnout. Breaks are normalized, resets are respected, and output is not valued over wellbeing.

      Psychological safety

      People should be able to speak up, ask for help, or admit uncertainty without fear of judgment.

      Different processing is valid

      People process pressure, conflict, and feedback differently. The culture makes room for that instead of punishing it.

      04

      Creative freedom, responsible tech

      Freedom without responsibility is noise. Responsibility without freedom is burnout. AI is a tool; people give it meaning. We use technology for drafting, enhancing, prototyping, testing, and building better creative systems.

      05

      Tech on the frontlines

      Engineers are co-creators. Architecture is design. Code is creative. We build film and tech together, with every sprint connected to story, user experience, and real ownership.

      06

      How we work together

      • No hero culture: we conduct as a team and focus on shared outcomes.
      • Progress beats perfection, but ownership still includes follow-through.
      • We explore, prototype, test, and improve instead of only implementing instructions.
      • Retrospectives, user feedback, editors, actors, and viewers can all shape better tools and stories.
      07

      Anti-values

      Micromanagement Office politics Toxic positivity Overwork as a badge Feedback hoarding Hidden rules Escalation before conversation
      Living document Culture is reviewed yearly, open to suggestions, and updated transparently. Questions: organizational@the-banished.com.

      Internship

      For interns

      Start here

      This section is for team members completing an internship with The Banished Inc. Every step below opens into the practical detail an intern needs before, during, and after the program.

      01

      Offer accepted

      Confirm the internship, start date, location, manager, and program track.

      Before anything else

      • Reply to the offer email confirming the start date, expected end date, work location, and weekly schedule.
      • Confirm your legal name, preferred name, phone number, and emergency contact with HR.
      • Ask HR which documents must be completed before your first paid workday.
      02

      Documents and signatures

      Complete every required signature before systems and production work are opened.

      Professional signing checklist

      • Sign the offer letter or internship confirmation, internship agreement, NDA, confidentiality terms, IP assignment, and handbook acknowledgment.
      • Complete payroll and tax forms such as W-4, I-9, direct deposit, local equivalents, and any school credit paperwork that applies.
      • Review code of conduct, anti-harassment, device responsibility, data security, and project confidentiality acknowledgments.
      • Submit signatures through the approved signing link only. Do not email unsigned personal documents unless HR specifically requests it.
      • HR confirms completion. Missing signatures can delay email, equipment, repository access, and assignment to confidential projects.
      03

      Corporate access

      Activate company email, portal access, collaboration tools, and basic security.

      Access rules

      • Use only your The Banished Inc. company email for portal access, company files, requests, and approvals.
      • Set up multi-factor authentication, password manager access, calendar, chat, file storage, and required project tools.
      • Report blocked access through IT Requests with screenshots, device type, browser, and urgency.
      04

      Equipment and setup

      Make sure the working environment is ready before the first assignment.

      Setup standard

      • Confirm whether you use a company device, a loaner, or an approved personal device.
      • Install approved software only and keep production files inside company-approved storage.
      • Test camera, audio, screen sharing, VPN if required, design tools, document access, and repository access before day one.
      05

      First day orientation

      Meet the company, understand the culture, and learn how decisions are made.

      Day one agenda

      • Meet HR, your manager, mentor, and the team members you will interact with most.
      • Review company culture, communication norms, confidentiality expectations, and the difference between questions, blockers, and decisions.
      • Confirm working hours, meeting expectations, time tracking if required, and how to request time away.
      06

      Week one work plan

      Turn the internship into clear deliverables, milestones, and feedback points.

      What good alignment includes

      • Agree on one primary learning objective, one practical deliverable, and one stretch opportunity.
      • Document success criteria, expected check-ins, review dates, and what must be done before asking for approval.
      • Keep a running work log with links, decisions, open questions, and blockers.
      07

      Mentor rhythm

      Use the mentor relationship for context, judgment, and practical learning.

      How to use your mentor well

      • Bring specific questions, drafts, options, or decisions. Mentors help faster when the request is concrete.
      • Ask for context before assuming how a creative, technical, or production standard works.
      • Escalate blockers that sit longer than one business day to both mentor and manager.
      08

      Midpoint review

      Check progress early enough to improve the outcome.

      Review preparation

      • Prepare a short summary of completed work, what changed, what you learned, and where you need clearer direction.
      • Your manager reviews quality, communication, reliability, judgment, and fit with the assigned work.
      • Agree on what must be finished, improved, or stopped before the final review.
      09

      Final presentation

      Close the internship with useful work, clear learning, and documented handoff.

      Final package

      • Present the final deliverable, key decisions, unresolved questions, files, and recommended next steps.
      • Clean up drafts, rename files clearly, and make sure another team member can continue the work.
      • Include a one-page reflection on skills built, feedback received, and what you would improve next time.
      10

      Conversion review

      Understand how return offers, references, and future opportunities are evaluated.

      What leadership considers

      • Business need, role availability, quality of work, communication, reliability, confidentiality, and growth potential.
      • Managers may recommend a return offer, extended internship, freelance project, reference, or no immediate next step.
      • HR communicates the outcome and any next actions after manager and leadership review.
